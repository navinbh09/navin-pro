Topology Optimization of Cold-Plate Flow Channels for Battery Thermal Management

SURGE Program, Undergraduate Researcher · May 2026 – Present · PI: Dr. Xianglin Li · Washington University in St. Louis

Overview

Batteries need to stay around 15–40 °C with less than 5 °C of variation across the pack. Cold plates force a trade-off: straight channels lose little pressure but cool unevenly, while serpentine channels cover the plate but lose pressure at every turn. This project asks whether adjoint-based topology optimization, which decides cell by cell where the channel walls go, can escape that trade-off. Simulations run in OpenFOAM v2512 on the McKelvey Engineering Research Compute Cluster.

Method

  • Validated the CFD setup against Ghia et al. (1982): lid-driven cavity at Re = 100 on a 150×150 mesh, with centerline velocities within 0.4% of the lid speed.
  • Characterized a 2D four-pass serpentine channel from Re ≈ 1,000 to 10,000: pressure drop rose 73× for a 10× increase in Re (42.2 to 3,072 Pa), close to Re², so the turns cause most of the loss.
  • Set up and debugged OpenFOAM's adjoint topology optimization framework (porosity-based Brinkman penalization, steady laminar SIMPLE, adjointOptimisationFoam, Method of Moving Asymptotes). First tested in 3D (72,000 cells, 60 cores, 70 stable cycles); the results below use 2D meshes.
  • It did not work off the shelf. Unconverged flow at Re = 1,000 turned the sensitivities into noise (fix: Re = 100); a flat start stalled the optimizer for about 20 cycles (fix: start at α = 0.3); the default move limit overshot and closed the channel (fix: 0.1, which converged smoothly in about 40 cycles).
  • Built OpenFOAM workflows spanning mesh generation (blockMesh, snappyHexMesh), solver configuration, turbulence modeling, and ParaView post-processing.

Results

  • Rounded turns (2D characterization study, not optimization): 0.5, 1.0 and 1.25 cm fillets cut pressure drop 12–17% in k-ω SST runs (Re ≥ 3,000), but laminar runs (Re ≤ 2,300) showed up to +14%. The sign flip sits exactly at the turbulence-model switch, so I treat it as an open question, not a result.
  • Optimization (2D, laminar, Re = 100): a single S-shaped channel cut pressure loss 19% vs. an open plate, using 40% of the domain as fluid.
  • With the whole plate free, pressure loss fell 87%, but coolant reached only 16% of the plate. That told me pressure alone is the wrong objective. There is no thermal objective yet, so these designs are not claimed to cool better.

Presentations & recognition

  • Poster: "Topology Optimization of Cold-Plate Flow Channels for Rechargeable Battery Thermal Management," Fall 2026 Undergraduate Research Symposium, WashU, Nov 6, 2026.
  • Five-minute lightning talk on this work, ASME St. Louis Section Mini Symposium, Nov 15, 2026. Awarded Best Talk by an Undergraduate Student.

Next steps

Verify the designs with solid walls, add peak-temperature and uniformity objectives, move to 3D plates, then 3D-print and test them.

Dye-Sensitized Solar Cells Using Natural-Dye Sensitizers

Research Assistant, Department of Physics · Feb 2024 – Aug 2025 · PI: Dr. Rasanjali Jayathissa · Truman State University

Overview

Investigated beetroot- and sumac-derived natural dyes as sustainable alternatives to conventional metal-complex sensitizers in dye-sensitized solar cells (DSSCs) — a lower-cost, more sustainable path to solar energy conversion than traditional silicon panels or synthetic sensitizers.

Method

  • Designed experiments for natural-dye extraction and characterization using UV-Vis, Fourier Transform Infrared (FTIR), and Electrochemical Impedance (EIS) spectroscopy.
  • Fabricated and tested 30 laboratory-scale DSSCs using solar-simulator I–V measurements.

Findings

  • The best-performing sumac-based cell achieved 0.099% efficiency and a 55.2% fill factor.
  • Linked higher photocurrent in sumac-based cells to stronger light absorption, and higher open-circuit voltage in beetroot-based cells to lower recombination.
  • Presented findings at the 2025 APS Prairie Section Meeting (Feb. 21, 2025).
