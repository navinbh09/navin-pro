Flow-Channel Optimization for Heat & Mass Transfer

SURGE Program, Undergraduate Researcher · May 2026 – Present · Advisor: Dr. Xianglin Li · Washington University in St. Louis

Overview

This project applies computational fluid dynamics and topology optimization to design flow-channel architectures that improve heat and mass transfer performance — work that sits at the intersection of thermal-fluid systems, numerical methods, and computational mechanics.

Method

  • Built OpenFOAM workflows spanning geometry creation, mesh generation, solver configuration, and convergence monitoring.
  • Post-processed and visualized flow & temperature fields in ParaView.
  • Implemented topology optimization using the Method of Moving Asymptotes (MMA) to generate optimized channel geometries.

Evaluation

  • Designs assessed on pressure drop, temperature distribution, heat-transfer performance, and thermal uniformity.
  • Computational models validated against published benchmark studies.

Status

Active, ongoing research — results and figures will be added here as the project progresses.

Dye-Sensitized Solar Cells Using Natural-Dye Sensitizers

Research Assistant, Department of Physics · Feb 2024 – Aug 2025 · Advisor: Dr. Rasanjali Jayathissa · Truman State University

Overview

Investigated plant-derived natural dyes as alternative sensitizers for dye-sensitized solar cells (DSSCs) — a lower-cost, more sustainable path to solar energy conversion than traditional silicon panels or synthetic sensitizers.

Method

  • Designed experiments for natural-dye extraction and characterization using UV-Visible spectroscopy and Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR).
  • Fabricated and tested 30+ laboratory-scale DSSCs.
  • Characterized performance using Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy (EIS) and current-voltage measurements from a solar simulator (max PCE ≈ 9.90%, 55% FF).

Findings

  • Analyzed relationships among dye properties, material characteristics, and photovoltaic performance.
  • Presented findings at the 2025 APS Prairie Section Meeting.

Acknowledgment

I gratefully acknowledge the mentorship and guidance of Dr. Rasanjali Jayathissa during this research, conducted at Truman State University.
